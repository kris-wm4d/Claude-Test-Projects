Dental implant options

A dental implant replaces a missing tooth's root and may support a crown, bridge or denture. An evaluation helps determine what may fit your needs.

A new foundation for replacement teeth

An implant replaces the root of a missing tooth and can support a custom restoration.

A dental implant is a small post, often made of titanium, that is placed in the jaw. As the area heals, the post can bond with the surrounding bone and provide support for a replacement tooth.

Depending on how many teeth are missing and your treatment plan, an implant may support a single crown, a bridge or a denture. Your dentist can explain the alternatives and what each option involves.

See how treatment is planned

One missing tooth or several

The right restoration depends on the number and position of missing teeth, your oral health and your preferences.

Replace a single tooth

An implant can support an individual crown where a tooth is missing, if your dentist determines it is appropriate.

Restore a larger space

For more than one missing tooth, an implant-supported bridge or another restoration may be considered.

Discuss every alternative

Bridges and removable dentures may also be options. Your consultation can compare the benefits, limits and care needs of each.

Your treatment, step by step

Planning is tailored to your mouth and general health. The number of visits and healing time vary from person to person.

  1. Evaluation and planning

    Your dentist reviews your health history and examines your mouth. Imaging may be used to assess the jaw and plan treatment.

  2. Implant placement

    If you proceed, the implant is placed in the jaw. Your clinician will discuss anesthesia, aftercare and what to expect during healing.

  3. Custom restoration

    Once healing allows, a crown, bridge or denture can be attached. Your dentist will explain how to care for it and arrange follow-up.

Is age a factor?

Age alone does not determine whether someone can receive an implant. Overall health, gum condition, available bone, medications and other factors may affect treatment.

A dentist can review your medical and dental history, discuss any concerns and tell you whether an implant or another option may be suitable.

Designed for everyday life

Implant-supported teeth are made to restore appearance and function, but they do not feel exactly like natural teeth. Your experience depends on your treatment and healing.

Daily cleaning and regular dental visits remain important. At a consultation, ask about maintenance, possible risks and the long-term care your restoration may need.

Questions about dental implants

How do I know if implants are right for me?

A dentist needs to examine your mouth and review your health history. Bone and gum health, medications and other factors can affect candidacy.

How long does treatment take?

Timing varies. Some people need additional treatment or healing time before a restoration can be attached. Your dentist can outline a schedule for your case.

Will an implant look like my other teeth?

A custom restoration is designed to suit your mouth. Your dentist can explain what may be achievable and what factors could affect the result.

What happens at a consultation?

You can discuss your concerns, have your oral health assessed and review possible treatments, expected visits, risks and fees before deciding how to proceed.
